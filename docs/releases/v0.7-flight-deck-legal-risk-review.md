## Risk-level legend

| Level | Meaning (consequence × uncertainty) |
|---|---|
| Critical | Realistic compromise exposes credentials, raw conversation/memory/transcript content, or PII at scale; or a release blocker independent of law. Must be resolved before the affected surface ships. |
| High | Material exposure of sensitive operational data or credentials on a credible failure path; or a legal/terms obligation that is release-gating. Must have a concrete control and a release gate. |
| Moderate | Meaningful but bounded exposure or a governance gap; mitigate with a documented control; approve at design/implementation review. |
| Low | Non-sensitive or aggregated data, or standard hygiene. |
| Unknown / insufficient evidence | Cannot assess without more facts (jurisdiction, terms, third-party data); treat as a gate until resolved. |

## 1. Issue

The v0.7.0 "Flight Deck" architecture and release plan introduce, relative to v0.6: durable encrypted token storage (D1-A), an owner-gated review-accept writer (D3-A), a unified read-only event-history surface (D4-A), restart reconciliation (ADR-007), hardened trusted-client access (OAuth promotion), and cross-machine seam interfaces (stretch). This review answers: **what legal, compliance, privacy, and security risks do these changes create or fail to mitigate, what are the required mitigations and escalation triggers, and what are the explicit go/no-go recommendations for gates G1–G5?**

The v0.6 risk posture (P0-1..P0-4, P1-1..P1-4, §6 cross-cutting controls, §8 retention, §9 ESCALATE items) is the baseline; v0.7 must not regress it. **The v0.6 ESCALATE items (jurisdiction determination, OpenAI/ChatGPT terms verification, third-party PII determination) remain open and are not cleared by anything in this review.**

## 2. Known facts (verified, point-in-time 2026-08-15)

Design under review (architecture/ADRs/risk register/implementation plan; committed 3bdbe61):

- **D1-A durable token store** (`token_store.py`): AES-256-GCM envelope at `<hermes_data>/secrets/hermes_gpt_tokens.json` (0600), plaintext per-client `{issuer, client_id, access_token, refresh_token, expires_at, scope, resource}` never on disk in the clear; key precedence OS keyring → key file `<hermes_data>/secrets/hermes_gpt_token_key` (0600) → env `HERMES_GPT_TOKEN_MASTER_KEY` (CI/test, "documented weakest"); rotation via `kid`; revocation deletes envelope/optionally rotates key via owner-gated `hermes_oauth_revoke` (pending legal). No token material in audit or MCP responses. **Subject to legal review before shipping (risk R4).**
- **D3-A review-accept writer** (`operator_review.py` / `hermes_review_accept`): owner level + direct + confirm + audit; writes `ReviewAcceptanceRecord` to `<hermes_data>/review-evidence/review-acceptances.jsonl` (append-only, atomic, 0600); `reviewer != assignee` enforced at write and re-checked at validate; evidence referenced (`evidence_refs`), never copied; raw prompts/transcripts never stored.
- **D4-A event-history surface** (`operator_events.py` / `hermes_events_query|tail`): read-model aggregator over operator audit JSONL, swarm-workflows JSON, codex-jobs JSON, cron executions.db, kanban task_events; no parallel write path; `read_only` + allowlist `HERMES_GPT_EVENTS_ALLOWED_SOURCES` (unset=all / list=only / empty=none — same semantics as Mission Control, and **not** to be called "deny by default"); retention window `HERMES_GPT_EVENTS_MAX_AGE_DAYS` default 90 (legal review); Mission Control redaction invariants reused (no raw bodies).
- **ADR-007 restart reconciliation** (`operator_recovery.py`): marks interrupted swarm stages `blocked` with `reason: interrupted_by_restart`; reloads token envelope; **never auto-advances or auto-dispatches**; `hermes_swarm_stage_advance` becomes idempotent for already-validated stages.
- **OAuth promotion** (P7): promote the unreleased OAuth boundary (commit 826aa43) to shipped/tested/documented; PKCE S256, 1h access / 30d rotating refresh with replay rejection, stateless signed auth codes, loopback/trusted-proxy enforcement; `server.py` already serves `protected_resource_metadata` and `authorization_server_metadata` endpoints and advertises `oauth2`/`bearer`/`noauth` security schemes.
- **New dependencies**: `cryptography` (required; installed 50.0.0), `keyring` (optional; installed 25.7.0).
- **Retention policy** (`docs/retention-policy.md`, current v0.6.0): request dumps 7d; Codex jobs 30d after terminal; swarm worktrees 7d; swarm workflow/verdict records 30d after terminal; **explicitly states it is "an operational retention policy, not a claim of legal or regulatory compliance"**; legal-hold exception exists.

Code facts verified (not taken on the design's word):

- `operator_policy.py` LEVELS = `["read_only", "cron", "skills", "skills_config", "workspace", "owner"]`; `DEFAULT_DENIED_BASENAMES` includes `.env`, `.env.local`, and secret-looking basenames; `SECRET_PATH_SUBSTRINGS` includes `"token"` — the new `secrets/` dir and `hermes_gpt_tokens.json`/`hermes_gpt_token_key` names are already denied to every tool by the existing path policy, which is the correct fail-closed posture (ADR-006 adds `secrets/` explicitly).
- `operator_contract.py` `_check_review` (lines 692–735) currently accepts exactly two evidence forms: (1) audit record of `hermes_contract_validate` by a reviewer distinct from the assignee; (2) human approval reference by someone other than the assignee. The new store becomes a third, backward-compatible evidence source.
- `oauth_auth.py` is memory-backed (`used_auth_codes`, `access_tokens`, `refresh_tokens` dicts; MAX_AUTH_CODES=1024, MAX_ACCESS_TOKENS=4096, MAX_REFRESH_TOKENS=4096; TTLs 300s/3600s/30d). Restart invalidates credentials today; D1-A changes this.
- `server.py` remote profile requires real auth unless both CLI flag `--i-understand-this-is-unsafe` **and** env `HERMES_GPT_UNSAFE_REMOTE_NOAUTH` are set (experiments only; not release-safe); `is_loopback_host` covers 127.0.0.1/localhost/::1.
- `LICENSE` is MIT. `pyproject.toml` deps: `mcp[cli]>=1.0,<2`, `packaging`, `uvicorn`, `tomli`; `cryptography`/`keyring` not yet added (planned S5).

External primary sources verified this session (2026-08-15):

## 3. Jurisdiction / governing law — unresolved dependency (carried from v0.6, still open)

Whether privacy law applies to this data flow, and which regime, **cannot be established from this matter** and materially changes compliance obligations. **ESCALATE.** Three facts drive the dependency (unchanged from v0.6 §3):

1. **Who the data is about.** Most surfaced data is Tony's own operational data, but some (messages, memories, cron outputs, event summaries derived from them) may reference **third-party individuals**. Third-party personal data attracts notice/consent and data-subject-rights duties under most regimes; operator-own data is far less regulated.
2. **Where Tony and any referenced individuals are located.** GDPR/UK GDPR (EU/UK), CCPA/CPRA (California consumers), other national/state laws. Not established; do not assume.
3. **Third-party processing relationship.** Tool results and event/review data flow to ChatGPT/OpenAI over the connector. This session verified the OpenAI data-controls position ("MCP servers are third-party services; data sent to an MCP server is subject to their data retention policies" — i.e., OpenAI's docs treat Tony's Hermes GPT as the third-party server whose policy governs data sent to it) and the App Developer Terms (separate controllers; security-measures duty; prohibitions on child data, PHI, PCI). **What remains unverified: Tony's account configuration (training opt-out/retention controls at the org/project level), the specific ChatGPT consumer terms for his account, and whether any surfaced data includes third-party PII.**

Until resolved, treat the ChatGPT data path as **Unknown / insufficient evidence** and gate v0.7 on the determinations in §7 rather than asserting compliance.

### 4.2 Security and secret handling

| # | Finding | Risk | Basis | Mitigation / gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Durable token store is the largest new secret-exposure surface.** D1-A converts memory-only credentials into an at-rest store. AES-256-GCM + 0600 + key separation is sound; but the **key-file fallback** (no keyring service) places the encryption key in the same `secrets/` directory as the ciphertext, so file-system-level compromise of the directory defeats the encryption. This is the design's own R4/R9 and it is correctly flagged. | **High (mitigable)** | ADR-001; architecture §6.4; risk register R4/R9; operator_policy SECRET_PATH_SUBSTRINGS | **Legal sign-off required before S5 ships** (already gated in plan). Conditions: (a) key-file fallback documented as materially weaker than keyring and disclosed as a leak window — never marketed as equivalent; (b) env master key used only for CI/test and documented weakest (already designed); (c) revocation path ships (owner-gated `hermes_oauth_revoke`) — see S3; (d) no token material in audit or MCP responses (already designed, assert by test); (e) dependency pin + CVE check for `cryptography`. |
| S2 | **`hermes_oauth_revoke` scope is pending legal sign-off.** Revocation is the control that makes "durable" credentials deletable — without it, a leaked or unwanted credential has no operator path to deletion except manual file removal. | **High (mitigable)** | Architecture §7.2; ADR-001; risk register checkpoint 3 | Legal sign-off on the owner-gated revoke surface before S5 ships. Recommendation: **approve** an owner+direct+confirm revoke tool that deletes the envelope (and optionally rotates the key), is audited, and returns no token material. Manual deletion is a poor fallback. |
| S3 | **OAuth promotion must not ship without live-connector verification.** The boundary is well-specified (PKCE S256, replay rejection, loopback/trusted-proxy) and aligned with the MCP 2025-11-25 authorization spec (RFC 9728/8414 discovery endpoints already served by server.py). But R2 (untested against live ChatGPT) is release-gating. | **High (ESCALATE if released without)** | Research §3.7; risk register R2; MCP spec 2025-11-25; server.py 1391–1398 | Mandatory live ChatGPT connector verification at G4 (already in plan). If it fails, the OAuth promotion slice is blocked; do not claim shipped compatibility. Also verify OIDC stays disabled and no OpenID Provider metadata leaks (already designed). |
| S4 | **Unsafe no-auth remote ack exists on master.** `--i-understand-this-is-unsafe` + `HERMES_GPT_UNSAFE_REMOTE_NOAUTH` bypasses auth for experiments only. It is pre-existing (not a v0.7 change) and gated by two explicit flags; AGENTS.md says "public unauthenticated Operator hosting is unsupported." | **Low** | server.py 38–39, 1725–1743; AGENTS.md invariants | Keep it out of the shipped OAuth path and out of release documentation as a supported mode; v0.7 docs must not present it as an option. No code change required. |
| S5 | **New dependency supply chain.** `cryptography` (Apache-2.0 OR BSD-3-Clause) and `keyring` (MIT) are permissive and license-compatible with the MIT repo; `cryptography` is a large, security-sensitive native dependency. | **Moderate** | pyproject.toml; PyPI/snyk license records; risk register R13 | Extend `test_package_hygiene.py` (planned); pin and CVE-scan `cryptography`; record the dependency decision in the ADR and package hygiene evidence at G3/G5. |
| S6 | **Token envelope plaintext fields are broad.** Storing `access_token` + `refresh_token` + `scope` + `resource` per client is necessary for restart-safe refresh, but it means a single decrypted envelope yields long-lived access. | **Moderate** | Architecture §6.4 | Mitigations already designed: 1h access / 30d rotating refresh with replay rejection; revocation; 0600. Consider documenting a max envelope age or refresh-token re-consent cadence as an operational control. |

## 5. Escalation triggers (concrete)

1. **R4/R9/R11 (token key management, secret paths, retention windows)** — any change to token key management, secret paths, or retention windows requires legal sign-off before the slice ships (G2/G4). If the key-file fallback is rejected as insufficient, escalate to default + legal for a keyring-only or hardware-key option.
2. **R3 (review independence)** — any proposed weakening of distinct-reviewer enforcement escalates to default + legal (product invariant: Work Contract validation fail-closed).
3. **R1 (CI)** — if Slice 0 cannot restore green CI within budget, block and escalate to default; do not weaken or skip safety tests.
4. **R2 (live connector)** — if live ChatGPT connector verification fails at G4, the OAuth promotion slice is blocked; do not claim shipped compatibility.
5. **P0-4 carryover** — before first live ChatGPT connector data transmission after release, escalate to counsel for jurisdiction/terms/third-party-PII determination (§7 checkpoint 4).
6. **Third-party client beyond Tony** — any future trusted client that is not Tony (or an entity Tony controls) requires contractual safeguards (DPA/confidentiality) before connecting (v0.6 P2-3; unchanged).

## 6. External counsel checkpoints

1. **Token storage + key management (ADR-001)** — before Slice 5 (S5) ships. Confirm key-file fallback posture, revocation scope, and dependency choice.
2. **Event retention window + review-evidence retention** — before S4/S3 ship. Confirm the effective event window (`min(source, 90d)` or chosen value) and review-evidence retention alignment with artifact lifecycles.
3. **`hermes_oauth_revoke` surface scope** — before S5 ships. Confirm owner-gated revoke is the right surface (recommended: yes).
4. **OpenAI/ChatGPT terms + jurisdiction + third-party PII (P0-4 carryover)** — before G5 / first live connector use after release. This session verified the OpenAI data-controls page and App Developer Terms; **what remains for counsel/Tony**: (a) Tony's account-level training/retention configuration under current terms, (b) applicable jurisdiction/regime for Tony and any referenced third parties, (c) whether any surfaced data contains third-party PII, (d) any notice/consent obligations before operational data is transmitted to ChatGPT.
5. **Positioning claims (growth lane)** — at G5: any claim must map to implemented capabilities; legal reviews the claim set for overstatement ("independent", "durable", "encrypted", "verified").
