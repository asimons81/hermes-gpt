## 1. Jurisdiction / applicable privacy regime (risk §9.1)

### What is established
Tony is in the **United States** (DreamHost DNS; Tailscale `heimdallstrategy.com`). The data is Tony's own operational data. There is no governing-law clause in any Hermes-side instrument (the Hermes tooling is Tony's own). The privacy obligations, if any, are statutory and depend on (a) who the data is about and (b) where those individuals reside.

### Analysis
- **Operator's-own operational data (no third-party personal data).** If the surfaced data contains **only Tony's own operational data** — profile names/models, fleet registry metadata, cron job names/schedules, task statuses, health, usage aggregates, redacted audit summaries — there is **no CCPA/GDPR/state-law duty to any third party**, because none of those laws protect an operator's own internal operational records about themselves. US federal privacy statutes (HIPAA, FERPA, GLBA, COPPA) do not apply to this data on their face (no health/student/financial-institution consumer data; no children's data). For this subset, the applicable "regime" is effectively none beyond Tony's own confidentiality/security obligations to providers under their terms. **This is the "likely minimal" case the risk review anticipated.**
- **CCPA/CPRA edge.** CCPA/CPRA protect "personal information" of **California residents**. It does not apply merely because Tony is in the US; it applies to personal information of California consumers. Tony is an individual operator, not a "business" meeting the CCPA's revenue/scale thresholds as a commercial actor collecting consumer PI, and the data is not consumer data gathered for commercial purposes. If, however, a surfaced free-text field contains personal information of a **California resident** (e.g. a name, email, or phone in an error string or audit summary), CCPA duties (notice, deletion/access rights, and the service-provider framing for the OpenAI onward flow) could attach to that incidental data.
- **GDPR/UK GDPR edge.** GDPR applies to personal data of individuals in the **EEA/UK** processed in connection with a controller/processor establishment or offering goods/services to, or monitoring, EU data subjects. Tony has no EU establishment; unless a surfaced field contains personal data of an **EU/UK data subject** (e.g. a European name/email referenced in a conversation-derived summary), GDPR does not apply. No such data is established from this matter.
- **Key dependency (item 3):** whether any surfaced field contains third-party personal data at all, and where those individuals reside. If yes and any are California residents or EU/UK residents, CCPA and/or GDPR duties attach to that incidental data regardless of Tony's own location.

### Bottom line for item 1
- **If operator's-own data only:** minimal-to-no US privacy regime applies; the practical duty is confidentiality/security and provider-term compliance, not notice/consent to data subjects.
- **If third-party PII appears:** CCPA is the most plausible US edge (California-resident data); GDPR/UK GDPR only if EU/UK-resident data appears. Both are conditionally applicable and would require notice/consent handling and a defensible basis for the onward OpenAI flow.

### 2.2 (a) May OpenAI use surfaced Hermes operational data for training?

**Answer depends on the account tier:**

**If the sidecar connects to CONSUMER ChatGPT (personal/individual account):** OpenAI **may use the Content for training by default**, subject to an opt-out.
- Terms of Use (effective Jan 1, 2026), "Our use of content": *"We may use Content to provide, maintain, develop, and improve our Services, comply with applicable law, enforce our terms and policies, and keep our Services safe."* And under "Opt out": *"If you do not want us to use your Content to train our models, you can opt out by following the instructions in this article."*
- "How your data is used to improve model performance" (updated Mar 13, 2026), "Services for individuals": *"When you use our services for individuals such as ChatGPT, Sora, or Operator, we may use your content to train our models."* and *"You can opt out of training through our privacy portal by clicking on 'do not train on my content.' ... Once you opt out, new conversations will not be used to train our models."*
- **Practical implication:** any operational data surfaced into a consumer ChatGPT conversation is, **by default, training data** unless (i) Tony turns off "Improve the model for everyone" / "do not train on my content" for the account, **and/or** (ii) the surfaced data is delivered via **Temporary Chat**, which per the same policy *"won't appear in history, use or create memories, or be used to train our models."* Temporary Chats are retained 30 days then deleted (see 2.3).

**If the sidecar connects via the BUSINESS/API path (API, ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT Business, Team):** OpenAI **does not train by default** and will not use Customer Content to improve services without explicit consent.
- Services Agreement (effective Jan 1, 2026), §4.2 "OpenAI Obligations": *"OpenAI will not use Customer Content to develop or improve the Services, unless Customer explicitly agrees to such use."*
- "How your data is used...", "Services for businesses": *"By default, we do not train on any inputs or outputs from our products for business users, including ChatGPT Team, ChatGPT Enterprise, and the API. ... Unless they explicitly opt-in, organizations are opted out of data-sharing by default."*
- Business data page: *"By default, we do not use data from ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT Edu, ChatGPT for Healthcare, ChatGPT for Teachers, or our API platform—including inputs or outputs—for training or improving our models."*
- **Practical implication:** under the business/API terms the no-training default holds; training would require an explicit opt-in (e.g. API Playground feedback sharing).

**Hermes-side note:** the inventory (S06) shows OpenAI Codex CLI (API-based) in use; the brief/design describe the ChatGPT client via tunnel/sidecar. Neither establishes whether the ChatGPT sidecar account is consumer or business. **This must be confirmed.**

### 2.3 (b) Retention windows

**Consumer ChatGPT:**
- Privacy Policy (updated Feb 6, 2026), §4 "Retention": *"We'll retain your Personal Data for only as long as we need in order to provide our Services to you, or for other legitimate business purposes such as resolving disputes, safety and security reasons, or complying with our legal obligations."* There is **no fixed retention window for ordinary conversations** — they persist until the user deletes them (delete requests are honored within 30 days: *"Once you choose to delete Personal Data, we will remove it from our systems within 30 days"*). Retention is indefinite until deletion for non-Temporary chats.
- Temporary Chats: *"will be automatically deleted within 30 days (unless we have to retain them for safety or legal reasons)"* (Privacy Policy §4; Data Controls FAQ).
- Longer retention is permitted for *"fraud and abuse, for security reasons, or for financial record-keeping purposes"* and to comply with legal obligations (Privacy Policy §4).

**API:**
- Data controls doc: *"As of March 1, 2023, data sent to the OpenAI API is not used to train or improve OpenAI models (unless you explicitly opt in)."* Abuse-monitoring logs *"retained for up to 30 days"*. Eligible customers can select **Zero Data Retention** or **Modified Abuse Monitoring** (ZDR forces `store=false` on supported endpoints).

**Business/Enterprise:**
- Enterprise privacy (updated Jan 8, 2026): *"You control how long your data is retained (ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT for Healthcare, ChatGPT Edu)"*; business-data page: *"We offer data retention controls for qualifying organizations ... including opting for our zero data retention policy in the API platform."* Business/Enterprise data is not trained on by default (§2.2).

**Net for retention:** ordinary consumer ChatGPT conversations have **no fixed window (indefinite until deleted)**; Temporary Chats are deleted at **30 days**; API abuse-monitoring logs are **up to 30 days**; business/Enterprise retention is customer-controllable (incl. zero-retention). The 30-day figure is not a general retention guarantee for consumer conversations.

### 2.4 (c) Does sending operational metadata to ChatGPT create a processor/sub-processor relationship requiring a DPA?

**Short answer: no separate DPA execution is required in either regime, but the legal character differs sharply by tier.**

**Consumer ChatGPT — NO processor relationship; OpenAI is a controller.** There is no DPA offered for consumer ChatGPT. Under the consumer Terms of Use and Privacy Policy, OpenAI processes Content as its **own controller** for its own purposes (improving the Services, training). Privacy Policy §11: for users outside the EEA/Switzerland, *"OpenAI OpCo, LLC ... is the controller and is responsible for the processing of your Personal Data."* Sending operational data to consumer ChatGPT therefore does **not** create a processor/sub-processor (Article 28 GDPR / service-provider) relationship and a DPA is **not** the operative instrument. The relationship is **controller-to-controller**, with OpenAI's consumer terms governing (including the default training use in §2.2). **Consequence:** Tony transmitting any third-party personal data to a controller on its own terms is a **disclosure**, and its lawfulness depends on notice/consent and lawful basis — not on a DPA. If the data is operator's-own only, no DPA and no data-subject duty attaches.

**Business/API — processor relationship governed by an incorporated DPA.** If the data path is the API or a business/Enterprise account and the data includes Personal Data, the Services Agreement and DPA apply:
- Services Agreement §5.3 "Privacy": *"If Customer uses the Services to process Personal Data, OpenAI and Customer will comply with the DPA, which is incorporated by this reference into the Agreement."*
- DPA §1.1: *"OpenAI acts as a Data Processor on the Customer's behalf, and this DPA governs such Processing."* DPA §1.2: OpenAI processes *"only for the purposes of delivering the Services"*; combined with Services Agreement §4.2 (no training without explicit consent).
- The DPA is **incorporated by reference**, so no separate signature/execution is required — it applies automatically under the business/API terms. It includes breach-notification (§2.7: *"OpenAI will notify Customer without undue delay after becoming aware of any Personal Data Breach"*), sub-processor list/notification (§2.9), and return/deletion on termination (§2.11).
- **Consequence:** under the business/API path, OpenAI is a **processor** when Personal Data is involved, governed by the incorporated DPA; a standalone DPA negotiation is not needed.

**Net for item 2:** the operative answer to (a) training, (b) retention, and (c) processor/DPA **all turn on whether the sidecar uses a consumer ChatGPT account or the business/API path**, plus (for consumer) whether training is opted out and whether surfaced data is delivered via Temporary Chat. No further OpenAI-term inspection is needed beyond confirming the account tier — the terms are now verified and cited.

## 3. Third-party PII determination (risk §9.3)

### Review of surfaced fields against the M0 view-model contracts (design §6.4)

The M0 design already implements the risk review's "no raw bodies / redact to summaries" constraint. Residual third-party PII exposure is therefore **not** in the bulk of surfaced data (profile names, fleet registry metadata, cron schedules, usage aggregates, health, vault metadata are all de-personalized metadata/aggregates). It is concentrated in **free-text fields that are truncated but not guaranteed PII-free**:

| Surface | Free-text fields surfaced | Third-party PII risk |
|---|---|---|
| `hermes_mission_failures` | `recent_errors[].message` — truncated error strings from logs, cron failures, kanban run errors, audit errors | **Highest.** Error strings commonly embed usernames, hostnames, emails, or prompt fragments. |
| `hermes_mission_audit` | `records[].summary` / `error` — the operator audit's human-readable summary | **Moderate-high.** Summaries can reference names, argv, or task context. Design redacts secret-looking paths and caps length but does not strip names/emails. |
| `hermes_mission_delegations` | `kanban_runs[].assignee`, `.outcome`, `.error` | **Moderate.** `assignee` is an internal profile name; `outcome`/`error` could reference third-party context. |
| `hermes_mission_cron` | `jobs[].name`, `.last_error` | **Moderate.** Job names and `last_error` could embed names/context. |
| `hermes_mission_approvals` | `approvals[].kind/.source/.id` — prompt **bodies redacted to `prompt_sha256`** | **Low** for PII (prompt body is hashed, not surfaced). |
| `hermes_mission_profiles/fleet/codex/health/vault/usage` | names, registry metadata, statuses, aggregates | **Low** — no free-text bodies. |

### Determination
- **Yes** — there is a realistic possibility that surfaced free-text fields (principally `failures.recent_errors[].message`, `audit.records[].summary/error`, `cron.jobs[].last_error`, `delegations.kanban_runs[].outcome/error`) contain **third-party personal data** — names, usernames, emails, or contact details of individuals referenced in operational context. The design mitigates by truncating and redacting secret-like material, but **truncation and secret-redaction do not guarantee removal of names/emails/domains**.
- **Which surfaces:** `failures` (highest), `audit`, `cron`, `delegations`. The `approvals`, `profiles`, `fleet`, `codex`, `health`, `vault`, and `usage` surfaces are effectively PII-clean under the current view-model (aggregates/metadata/hashes only).
- **Notice/consent:** If third-party PII is present, and the affected individual is a **California resident**, CCPA notice/consent/access-deletion duties attach; if an **EU/UK resident**, GDPR applies. Whether Tony has a defensible lawful basis (e.g. legitimate interests) and whether consent is required is a **counsel question**. If only Tony's own data is present, no notice/consent duty to third parties arises.

### Recommend (technical, reduces risk to near-zero)
Add a **PII-strip pass** in the Mission Control redaction layer for the four free-text surfaces above (strip or hash email addresses, phone numbers, usernames prefixed `@`, and common name patterns in `message`/`summary`/`error`/`last_error`/`outcome` fields before surfacing). This is a hardening of the existing no-raw-body control (risk review §6 cross-cutting 2), not a new exposure.

## 4. Breach-notification duties (risk §9.5)

One-paragraph note. If a Mission Control compromise (or an OpenAI-side incident on the surfaced data) exposed personal data, notification duties are **jurisdiction- and data-subject-location-dependent**. If the exposed data includes personal information of **California residents**, the CCPA's data-breach provisions (California Civ. Code §1798.82) require notification to affected residents without unreasonable delay (after the Attorney General's 45-day exception window where applicable, subject to law-enforcement delay), and, for larger breaches, notification to the California Attorney General and credit-reporting agencies. If **EEA/UK** data subjects are affected, GDPR/UK GDPR Article 33 requires notification to the relevant supervisory authority **within 72 hours** of becoming aware, and Article 34 notification to affected individuals where the risk to rights/freedoms is high. If the data is **operator's-own only** (no third-party personal data), most US-state and all GDPR breach-notification statutes do not attach (they are keyed to personal data of residents/individuals). Because the exact data-subject residency is not established, the definitive trigger cannot be stated here; the safe posture is (a) implement read-access logging (risk review cross-cutting 4, already gated) so a compromise is reconstructable, and (b) before any release confirm whether third-party PII can be present and, if so, retain counsel's breach-response plan covering the California 45-day and GDPR 72-hour regimes.
