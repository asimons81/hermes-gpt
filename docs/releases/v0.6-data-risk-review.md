## Risk-level legend

| Level | Meaning (consequence × uncertainty) |
|---|---|
| Critical | Realistic compromise exposes credentials, raw conversation/memory/transcript content, or PII at scale; consequences severe and likely if unmitigated. Must be resolved before release. |
| High | Material exposure of sensitive operational data or PII on a credible failure path; must have a concrete control and a release gate. |
| Moderate | Meaningful but bounded exposure (metadata, summaries, costs); mitigate with minimization/redaction; approve at design review. |
| Low | Non-sensitive or aggregated data; standard hygiene suffices. |
| Unknown / insufficient evidence | Cannot assess without more facts (often jurisdiction or third-party terms); treat as a gate until resolved. |

## 1. Issue

Mission Control (v0.6) will expose Hermes operational state — profiles, fleet agents, Codex jobs, cron activity, delegated work, failures, pending approvals, health, and additional candidate surfaces (Vault, memory, LCM, usage/cost, audit trail) — to **trusted clients** (ChatGPT first; a future authenticated read-only API). The question this review answers: **what could go wrong, what is the residual risk, and which controls / release gates must be in place before each sensitive surface ships?**

Per the approved brief: exposure is **read-only, authenticated, trusted-client only**. Non-goals NG3 (no unauthenticated/public exposure) and NG4 (no multi-tenant/SaaS) already constrain scope, which materially lowers risk and is presumed for the rest of this review. If those non-goals change, this review must be revisited.

## 3. Jurisdiction / governing law — unresolved dependency

Whether, and to what extent, privacy law applies to this data flow **cannot be established from this matter** and materially changes compliance obligations. **ESCALATE for counsel.** Until resolved, treat as **Unknown / insufficient evidence** and gate v0.6 on the determinations below rather than asserting compliance.

Three facts drive the dependency:

1. **Who the data is about.** Much of the exposed data is Tony's own operational data. Some surfaces (messages, memories, cron outputs, approval prompts, chat bodies) may contain personal data of **third parties** (individuals referenced in conversations). Personal data of third parties attracts notice/consent and data-subject-rights duties under most privacy regimes; purely operator's-own data is far less regulated in many jurisdictions.
2. **Where Tony (and any referenced individuals) are located.** Regimes such as GDPR/UK GDPR (if EU/UK relevant), state laws like CCPA/CPRA (California consumers), and other national/state laws impose different duties. No governing-law or residence basis is established in the task inputs. Do not assume.
3. **The third-party processor relationship with OpenAI/ChatGPT.** Surfacing operational data to ChatGPT means Tony's (and possibly third-party) data is transmitted to and processed by a third party (OpenAI) outside Hermes's control. Whether that is covered by OpenAI's terms, whether Tony is the controller/processor, and what retention/training terms apply depend on the current OpenAI/ChatGPT commercial terms and Tony's account. **These terms were not inspected and must be verified.**

## 4. Threat model & trust boundary

Mission Control introduces a **new, narrower trust boundary**: the trusted-client surface. It is internal to Tony's fleet, but the primary risk is not a malicious external attacker — it is:

- **(T1) The trusted client itself.** "Trusted" is a design label, not a security control. If the ChatGPT integration or the API authenticates a client that is later compromised, or authenticates insufficiently (single shared token, no scope, no per-surface authorization), the attacker inherits *everything* the surface exposes. Because the underlying stores are HIGH sensitivity (credentials, transcripts, memories, messages), a broken authZ boundary is a Critical risk regardless of "trusted client" wording.
- **(T2) Over-exposure / data minimization failure.** Aggregating raw bodies (messages, `async_delegations.result_json`, Codex transcripts, audit summaries, Vault metadata, interrupted-turn prompts) instead of redacted summaries directly leaks the most sensitive data even with correct authentication.
- **(T3) Secret leakage through a "read-only" surface.** Read-only does not mean non-sensitive. `hermes status` shows API keys/auth providers; profile dirs contain `auth.json`, `.env`; a2a config holds `auth_token`; MCP tokens exist. Any surface that returns or derives token material is Critical.
- **(T4) Third-party processing of personal data.** Transmitting PII to OpenAI without notice/consent or clear terms is a legal risk independent of technical controls (see §3).
- **(T5) Auditability gap.** If read access to sensitive surfaces is not itself logged, a misuse/incident cannot be reconstructed, weakening breach-response and accountability.
- **(T6) Injection / prompt-borne exfiltration via a trusted client.** A compromised or adversarial message inside a surfaced conversation could attempt to instruct the control-plane client to broaden access or exfiltrate. Control-plane clients must not be prompt-executable beyond their granted scope. (Relates to Outcome/Work-Contracts authority boundaries, which are a sibling capability, not this review's scope — but the principle applies.)

## 5. Applicable legal/policy regimes — framework to verify, not a conclusion

Presented as categories the design and plan must account for once jurisdiction and terms are established; none are asserted to apply here.

- **Privacy/data-protection (if in scope).** GDPR/UK GDPR (lawful basis, minimization, security, data-subject rights, cross-border transfer to a processor like OpenAI); US state consumer-privacy statutes (e.g. CCPA/CPRA, others) where a consumer is implicated; breach-notification duties if a compromise exposes personal data. **ESCALATE.**
- **Confidentiality / trade secrets / credentials.** Hermes `auth.json`, Vault credentials, A2A/MCP tokens, and SOUL prompts are sensitive trade/operational assets; exposure and misuse risk is a commercial/security matter independent of privacy law. This is a contractual/security obligation Tony owes himself (and, to providers, under their terms).
- **Third-party platform terms (OpenAI/ChatGPT, model providers).** Tony's use of ChatGPT/Codex/API providers is governed by their current terms. Whether the provider may process operational data, and whether that is permitted, must be verified against current terms before shipping. **ESCALATE / verify.**
- **Contractual safeguards for future trusted clients.** The v0.6 brief contemplates future programmatic clients. Those need an authenticated API, an access policy, and (where a client is a third party processing Tony's data) a data-processing/confidentiality agreement. Not needed for Tony-as-only-operator, but needed before any external third-party client connects.

The design (t_0100037c) and plan (t_9aa78bdd) must carry these as release gates, not just this document.

### Cross-cutting controls (apply to every surface)

1. **Authentication + per-surface authorization (authN/authZ).** Every trusted client authenticates (token/OAuth); authorization is **deny-by-default per surface**, scoped to what that client needs. No "authenticated = all surfaces" model. This is the design's (t_0100037c) security boundary. **Gate: no surface ships without authN + scoped authZ + read-access logging.**
2. **Redaction / summarization at the view boundary.** Build one redaction layer that all surfaces pass through; strip token material, message/prompt/transcript bodies, memory/fact content, and raw log lines. **Gate: a mandatory secret-redaction and no-raw-body test is a release-blocker for v0.6.**
3. **No raw bodies.** Raw message, transcript, prompt, memory, and request-dump content is **not approved** for any v0.6 surface. Where a summary is acceptable it must be opt-in and reviewed.
4. **Audit of read access (T5).** Log which client read which surface when. This is required to reconstruct misuse and to support breach response. **Gate: read-access logging required before release.**
5. **Tony notice/consent for third-party transmission.** Because Mission Control can push sensitive data to ChatGPT/OpenAI, Tony must be shown and approve (a) exactly which surfaces are exposed and to whom, and (b) the current OpenAI/ChatGPT data-usage terms, before the integration goes live. **Gate: explicit Tony approval at release.**

## 8. Retention & deletion

- **Request dumps (G9) and Codex transcripts (G8):** diagnostic-only, high-sensitivity. Recommend a defined retention window and deletion procedure (they are not operational-observability data). Decide owner and window in the plan (t_9aa78bdd). **Gate: retention policy documented before release.**
- **Aggregated view caches:** Mission Control should not durably cache raw-derived material longer than needed for a bounded refresh; cache summaries only. If it caches anything sensitive, define retention. **Gate: design review.**
- Do not treat this as a data-retention-legal analysis; if jurisdiction-specific retention duties arise (e.g. breach-notification recordkeeping), counsel should confirm.

## 9. What requires actual counsel (ESCALATE)

These are the decisions that need qualified professional judgment and cannot be resolved by this review:

1. **Jurisdiction / applicable privacy regime** for Tony and any third parties whose personal data may be in surfaced stores (messages, memories, cron outputs, approvals). This determines whether and which privacy duties apply (notice/consent, minimization, data-subject rights, breach notification, cross-border transfer).
2. **Third-party processing by OpenAI/ChatGPT** of any personal data transmitted to it, and whether current OpenAI terms/account configuration permit that processing and on what basis. Counsel or Tony must verify current terms (they were not inspected here).
3. **Whether any surfaced data constitutes third-party PII** requiring notice/consent and data-subject-rights handling, versus purely operator's-own data.
4. **Contractual safeguards** for any future third-party trusted client (DPA/confidentiality), should one be added outside Tony-as-sole-operator.
5. **Breach-notification duties** if a Mission Control compromise exposes personal data (jurisdiction-dependent).

Items 1–3 are **release-gating** for transmitting data to ChatGPT. Items 4–5 are conditionally relevant.
